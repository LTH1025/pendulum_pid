# Work Summary — A Personal MuJoCo Cart-Double-Pendulum Swing-Up Experiment

This folder records an assisted personal experiment in **MuJoCo (v3.12.0) on Ubuntu 24.04 (VMware)**,
carried out with the help of an AI assistant. The physical system studied here is a
**cart double pendulum** driven by a single horizontal force applied to the cart along a
linear guide rail (coordinate `x`), unlike a torque-actuated acrobot. The two-link chain
hangs jointed at the cart and at the middle joint, and the only control input is the
horizontal cart force.

---

## 1. Goal

Starting from the double pendulum hanging straight down, drive it **up to the fully
inverted (upright) position in one continuous motion** — without any full-circle rotations
or flinging — and then try to **hold it balanced upright**. The cart is free to travel
along `x`; no fixed set-point is required.

## 2. What was built and done (assistant-assisted)

### 2.1 Simulation model (`asset/pendulum.xml`)
Built and iterated on a MuJoCo XML scene: a cart constrained to a linear rail plus two
revolute-jointed links, with the horizontal cart force as the single actuator.

### 2.2 Controller design (`control_pendulum.py`)
Implemented a PD controller (“PID-control learning”, `Ki = 0`), where the torque/force is
computed in Python from the position and angular-velocity errors and applied to the cart.
Structured the control as state-feedback (
`-KP·x − KD·vx − DP1·dq1 …`) to pump energy during swing-up and to switch toward balancing.
Reached a swing-up-and-capture pipeline that reliably raises the links to the upright region,
with the best capture near perfect upright (small deviation, small angular velocity).

### 2.3 Simulation & visualization
Ran the closed-loop simulation in MuJoCo’s viewer and logged state/torque trajectories.
Confirmed the GUI/simulate fetch can only start from the VM desktop (no DISPLAY from a
headless CLI), and the passive viewer is used for inspection.
This folder’s **`Animation for the output.gif`** is the recorded animation of the run, and
**`The data output graph.png`** is the plot of the recorded state/torque data
(generated with Matplotlib under the `fig/` outputs of the main repo).
